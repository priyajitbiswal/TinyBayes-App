# TinyBayes: Comprehensive Crop Disease Remedies Reference Guide

This document provides agricultural management strategies, cultural practices, biological controls, and chemical treatments for all diseases supported by the TinyBayes offline crop disease classification models.

---

## 1. Cocoa (*Theobroma cacao*)

### 1.1 Anthracnose (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides* / *C. theobromicola*)
- **Symptoms**: Irregular brown necrotic lesions on leaves often starting from margins or tips; leaf blight and premature defoliation; pod spots that become sunken and necrotic.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Regular pruning of cocoa trees and shade trees to improve air circulation and direct sunlight penetration (aim for 30–50% canopy shade).
  - Practice field sanitation: collect and bury or burn infected pods, fallen leaves, and diseased twigs.
  - Avoid mechanical injury to leaves, pods, and cherelles during weeding and harvesting.
- **Biological & Organic Control**:
  - Foliar application of bio-control agents such as *Trichoderma harzianum* or *Bacillus subtilis* to colonize leaf surfaces.
  - Preventive sprays of neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%) or certified copper soap formulations at the onset of rain.
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Preventive**: Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5–3.0 g/L) or Cuprous Oxide applied every 2–3 weeks during flushing and rainy seasons.
  - **Curative/Systemic**: At early disease appearance, apply Tebuconazole 25.9% EC (1 ml/L), Difenoconazole 25% EC (0.5 ml/L), or Pyraclostrobin 20% WG. Rotate FRAC fungicide groups to prevent resistance.

---

### 1.2 Cocoa Swollen Shoot Virus Disease (CSSVD)
- **Symptoms**: Swelling of chupons, twigs, and roots; red vein-banding on young flushes; chlorotic vein-clearing, mosaic, and fern-leaf patterns on mature leaves; rounded, small pods.
- **Transmission**: Vectored by mealybugs (*Planococcoides njalensis*, *Ferrisia virgata*) and infected vegetative budwood.
- **Notice**: Viral infection; **no chemical cure** exists. Immediate containment and eradication are mandatory.
- **Primary Remedy & Eradication ("Cutting-Out")**:
  - Immediately cut out (uproot) all visibly infected trees along with neighboring contact trees within a 5–10 meter radius.
  - Burn or bury felled plant parts on-site to destroy the virus source and resident mealybugs.
- **Vector & Border Management**:
  - Suppress mealybug populations and attending ant colonies around boundary barriers using targeted systemic insecticides (e.g., Imidacloprid).
  - Establish 10-meter border barriers of immune crops (citrus, oil palm, rubber) around cocoa plots.
- **Replanting**:
  - Replant only with CSSVD-tolerant hybrid seedlings certified by agricultural extension services.
  - Maintain optimal shade (around 50%) to reduce tree physiological stress.

---

### 1.3 Healthy
- **Status**: The foliage shows healthy pigmentation and cell integrity.
- **Action**: No remedy required. Maintain balanced nutrition, proper pruning, and routine scouting.

## 2. Cotton (*Gossypium hirsutum*)

### 2.1 Bacterial Blight / Angular Leaf Spot (*Xanthomonas citri pv. malvacearum*)
- **Symptoms**: Small, water-soaked angular leaf spots bounded by veinlets; lesions turn reddish-brown then black; "black arm" lesions on petioles and stems causing dieback.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Sow certified disease-free, acid-delinted seeds.
  - Deep summer ploughing to bury and decompose crop residues.
  - Practice 2-year crop rotation with non-host crops (cereals, pulses).
  - Avoid overhead flood or sprinkler irrigation that splashes bacterial ooze.
- **Biological & Seed Treatment**:
  - Treat seeds with *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (10 g/kg seed) or soak in hot water (56°C for 10 minutes).
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Seed Treatment**: Streptocycline (100 ppm) + Copper Oxychloride (2 g/kg seed).
  - **Foliar Spray**: Spray Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5 g/L) tank-mixed with Streptocycline or Plantomycin (100 mg/L) at first symptom onset. Repeat after 12–15 days if wet, humid weather persists.

---

### 2.2 Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuD)
- **Symptoms**: Upward or downward curling of leaf margins; thickened leaf veins; leafy enation outgrowths on the lower leaf surface; severe plant stunting and reduced boll setting.
- **Transmission**: Transmitted by the sweetpotato whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*).
- **Notice**: Viral infection; **no direct chemical cure**. Focus strictly on vector management and eradication of early-infected plants.
- **Cultural Management**:
  - Rogue out and burn diseased plants within 45–60 days of sowing.
  - Keep field margins, bunds, and water channels free of weed reservoirs (*Abutilon indicum*, *Sida cordifolia*, *Parthenium*).
  - Sow CLCuV-resistant or tolerant hybrid cotton cultivars.
- **Vector Control (Whitefly)**:
  - Install yellow sticky traps (15–20 traps/acre) for population monitoring and suppression.
  - Spray Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%) or Neem oil (10,000 ppm at 2 ml/L) during early vegetative stages.
  - Apply systemic insecticides at Economic Threshold Level: Diafenthiuron 50% WP (1.2 g/L), Spiromesifen 22.9% SC (1 ml/L), Pyriproxyfen 10% EC (2 ml/L), or Flonicamid 50% WG (0.3 g/L). Avoid pyrethroids which induce whitefly resurgence.

---

### 2.3 Herbicide Growth Damage
- **Symptoms**: Leaf strapped into fan-shaped/feathered forms; parallel veins; blistering and downward twisting of petioles; bud suppression (commonly caused by synthetic auxin drift like 2,4-D or dicamba).
- **Notice**: Abiotic chemical injury; **cannot be reversed with pesticides**. Nutritional/hormonal recovery sprays do not restore damaged leaves.
- **Agronomic Recovery & Management**:
  - Check the apical terminal bud: If the terminal bud is alive and newly formed leaves appear normal, the plant will outgrow moderate injury.
  - Ensure steady soil moisture through regulated irrigation to prevent moisture stress (avoid waterlogging).
  - Supply light foliar nutrition (1% 19:19:19 NPK or 1% urea) once new growth starts to assist canopy rebuilding.
  - Manage as a late-planted crop: Adjust pest scouting and harvest scheduling to compensate for delayed boll maturity.
  - Clean and flush all spray equipment thoroughly before spraying future fields.

---

### 2.4 Leaf Hopper / Jassids (*Amrasca biguttula biguttula*)
- **Symptoms**: Downward curling of leaf margins; yellowing followed by triangular red-brown marginal necrosis ("hopper burn"); leaves become brittle and drop prematurely.
- **Cultural & Biological Control**:
  - Cultivate hairy/pubescent cotton varieties that deter jassid oviposition and feeding.
  - Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization, which creates soft, succulent foliage.
  - Install yellow sticky traps (10–15 traps/acre) at crop canopy level.
  - Conserve natural predators: ladybird beetles (*Coccinella* spp.) and green lacewings (*Chrysoperla carnea*).
- **Chemical Control (ETL: 1–2 nymphs/leaf)**:
  - Flonicamid 50% WG (0.3 g/L) or Dinotefuran 20% SG (0.3 g/L).
  - Imidacloprid 17.8% SL (0.3 ml/L) or Thiamethoxam 25% WG (0.2 g/L). Rotate chemistries to prevent resistance.

---

### 2.5 Leaf Reddening ("Lalya")
- **Cause**: Physiological disorder caused by environmental stresses (chilling night temperatures <21°C, drought, or waterlogging) triggering anthocyanin accumulation, exacerbated by Magnesium (Mg) and Nitrogen (N) deficiency.
- **Notice**: Non-pathogenic physiological disorder; red leaves cannot turn back green, but further spread to developing leaves can be prevented.
- **Immediate Foliar Remedy**:
  - Spray Magnesium Sulphate ($MgSO_4$) at 10–20 g/L (1–2%) combined with 1% Urea (10 g/L) or 2% DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate).
  - Repeat foliar spray after 10–12 days during peak boll development.
- **Soil & Cultural Management**:
  - Apply 25 kg/ha Magnesium Sulphate to the soil during basal fertilizer application in endemic zones.
  - Maintain good field drainage to prevent water stagnation around root zones.
  - Maintain light, regular irrigations during sudden temperature drops.

---

### 2.6 Leaf Variegation
- **Cause**: Genetic mutation or chimera causing localized deficiency of chlorophyll in patches or stripes.
- **Notice**: Harmless, non-pathogenic condition. It does not spread across plants and does not diminish yield.
- **Remedy**:
  - No chemical or biological treatment is required or beneficial.
  - Maintain standard irrigation and crop fertility.
  - In subsequent seasons, use certified seeds from certified seed producers.

---

### 2.7 Healthy Leaf
- **Status**: Leaf displays normal green chlorophyll pigmentation and healthy physiological vigor.
- **Action**: No remedy required. Maintain optimal irrigation, balanced fertilization, and routine pest scouting.

## 3. Potato (*Solanum tuberosum*)

### 3.1 Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*)
- **Symptoms**: Dark brown to black circular lesions on older leaves exhibiting concentric rings ("target board" or "bullseye" appearance), bordered by chlorotic halos; premature leaf drop.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Practice 2–3 year crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops (cereals, legumes, corn).
  - Use furrow or drip irrigation to avoid leaf wetness; avoid late afternoon/evening irrigation.
  - Maintain proper row spacing to ensure adequate canopy airflow.
  - Prune and safely bury infected lower leaves displaying initial lesions.
- **Biological & Organic Control**:
  - Apply bio-fungicides such as *Bacillus subtilis* or *Trichoderma viride* (2.5 g/L) preventively.
  - Foliar sprays of copper soap (copper octanoate) at 7–10 day intervals.
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Preventive**: Mancozeb 75% WP (2.0–2.5 g/L) or Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L) every 7–10 days before canopy closure.
  - **Curative/Systemic**: At first sighting of lesions, apply Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC (1 ml/L), Tebuconazole 25.9% EC (1 ml/L), or Pyraclostrobin 20% WG.

---

### 3.2 Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*)
- **Symptoms**: Water-soaked, pale to dark lesions rapidly expanding across leaves; white cottony/frosty mold sporulation on the underside of leaves under humid conditions; rapid stem rot and tuber blight.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Plant only certified disease-free seed tubers.
  - Completely destroy all volunteer potato plants and cull piles before planting.
  - Ensure deep hilling (10–15 cm soil cover) over potato hills to prevent washed-down zoospores from contacting tubers.
  - De-haulming: Cut or chemically desiccate vines 10–14 days before harvest if late blight is active in the canopy to prevent tuber inoculation during digging.
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Preventive (High humidity/Cool weather)**: Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L) or Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (3.0 g/L).
  - **Curative (Immediate outbreak response)**: Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb (Ridomil Gold, 2.5 g/L), Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (2.0 g/L), Dimethomorph 50% WP (1.0 g/L), or Mandipropamid 23.4% SC (0.8 ml/L). Alternate fungicide modes of action.

---

### 3.3 Healthy
- **Status**: Potato foliage is uniform, vigorous, and free from foliar blights or viral deformations.
- **Action**: No remedy required. Continue preventive scouting, proper hilling, and regulated irrigation.

---

## 4. Rice (*Oryza sativa*)

### 4.1 Bacterial Leaf Blight (*Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae*)
- **Symptoms**: Water-soaked stripes along leaf margins starting from tips; lesions turn yellow to bleached white with wavy margins; milky bacterial droplets may ooze on leaf surfaces early in the morning.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization. Apply nitrogen in 3–4 split doses based on the Leaf Color Chart (LCC).
  - Temporarily drain standing water from the field for 3–5 days to slow down bacterial proliferation.
  - Avoid clipping rice seedlings during transplanting.
  - Maintain clean bunds and eliminate host grass weeds (*Leersia hexandra*, *Echinochloa*).
- **Biological & Chemical Control**:
  - Foliar spray of *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (2 g/L or 1 kg/ha) at 30 and 45 days after transplanting.
  - Spray Copper Hydroxide 77% WP (2 g/L) or Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5 g/L) combined with Streptocycline (100–150 ppm, 1–1.5 g per 10 L of water). Repeat after 10–12 days if rains continue.

---

### 4.2 Brown Spot (*Bipolaris oryzae*)
- **Symptoms**: Numerous oval, sesame-seed-shaped dark brown spots with gray or whitish centers and yellow halos across leaf blades and glumes. Often indicates soil nutrient exhaustion.
- **Cultural & Agronomic Control**:
  - Correct soil nutritional deficits: Apply recommended Potash (MOP), Zinc Sulphate, and Silicon amendments.
  - Maintain continuous shallow water in the field; avoid drought stress.
  - Sow certified healthy seeds.
- **Seed & Chemical Treatment**:
  - **Seed Treatment**: Carbendazim 50% WP (2 g/kg seed) or hot-water soak (53–54°C for 10–12 minutes).
  - **Foliar Spray**: Spray Propiconazole 25% EC (1 ml/L), Hexaconazole 5% SC (2 ml/L), or Tricyclazole 18% + Mancozeb 62% WP (2.5 g/L) at boot leaf stage and 50% heading.

---

### 4.3 Leaf Blast (*Magnaporthe oryzae* / *Pyricularia oryzae*)
- **Symptoms**: Spindle-shaped or eye-shaped lesions with brown borders and whitish-gray centers; lesions coalesce, causing complete blighting ("scorched" leaf canopy appearance).
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Avoid heavy, late nitrogen applications; ensure balanced N:P:K ratios with adequate potassium.
  - Maintain a steady shallow water layer (2–5 cm) in paddy fields to mitigate stress.
  - Sow blast-tolerant/resistant cultivars.
- **Biological Control**:
  - Seed treatment with *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (10 g/kg seed) followed by seedling root dip (2.5 kg/ha).
- **Chemical Control**:
  - Spray Tricyclazole 75% WP (0.6 g/L or 300–400 g/ha) at the earliest detection of spindle-shaped lesions.
  - Alternative chemistries: Isoprothiolane 40% EC (1.5 ml/L), Kasugamycin 3% SL (2–2.5 ml/L), or Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC (1 ml/L).

---

### 4.4 Leaf Scald (*Microdochium oryzae*)
- **Symptoms**: Conspicuous zonate, water-soaked bands starting from leaf tips or margins; alternating light tan and dark brown chevron-like bands resembling scald burns.
- **Cultural & Chemical Control**:
  - Provide adequate seedling spacing to ensure cross-canopy sunlight and aeration.
  - Avoid excessive nitrogen doses, particularly during overcast, cloudy monsoon spells.
  - Spray Propiconazole 25% EC (1 ml/L), Carbendazim 12% + Mancozeb 63% WP (1.5–2.0 g/L), or Benomyl 50% WP (1.0 g/L) at boot stage or early panicle emergence.

---

### 4.5 Narrow Brown Spot (*Cercospora janseana*)
- **Symptoms**: Short, linear, narrow dark brown lesions (2–10 mm long, 1 mm wide) running parallel to leaf veins; causes premature leaf death and lodging late in the season.
- **Cultural & Chemical Control**:
  - Apply recommended potassium fertilizer (potash deficiency drastically increases susceptibility).
  - Practice field sanitation and crop rotation where the disease is persistent.
  - Spray Propiconazole 25% EC (1 ml/L) or Azoxystrobin 23% SC (1 ml/L) between late panicle differentiation and early heading.

---

### 4.6 Healthy
- **Status**: Rice leaf shows vibrant green color, strong turgidity, and no signs of bacterial or fungal blights.
- **Action**: No remedy required. Maintain optimal water levels, balanced fertilization, and regular field scouting.

---

## 5. Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)

### 5.1 Bacterial Spot (*Xanthomonas perforans* / *X. vesicatoria*)
- **Symptoms**: Small (less than 3 mm), dark brown, water-soaked circular spots with greasy texture on leaves; spots coalesce, causing leaf scorch and defoliation; blister-like raised scab lesions on fruit.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Use certified disease-free seed or hot-water treated seed (50°C for 25 minutes).
  - Prune lower canopy foliage and mulch heavily with organic or plastic mulch to stop soil splashing.
  - Avoid overhead irrigation; work in crop beds only when leaves are completely dry.
  - Rotate crops away from solanaceous plants for at least 2 years.
- **Biological Control**:
  - Apply bacteriophage formulations (e.g., AgriPhage) or *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* strain D747 preventively.
- **Chemical Control**:
  - Spray Copper Hydroxide 53.8% WG (2.0 g/L) or Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5 g/L) tank-mixed with Mancozeb 75% WP (2.0 g/L). Tank-mixing with mancozeb is vital to break copper resistance in bacterial spot populations.

---

### 5.2 Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*)
- **Symptoms**: Dark brown to black circular lesions on older lower foliage exhibiting distinct concentric rings ("target boards") with surrounding chlorosis; causes leaf drop from bottom upwards.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Stake plants and prune lower suckers up to 30–45 cm above the soil line for enhanced air circulation.
  - Apply clean straw or plastic mulch around the plant base.
  - Drip irrigate at root zones; keep foliage dry.
  - Rotate crops with non-solanaceous species for 2–3 years.
- **Biological & Organic Control**:
  - Preventive sprays of *Bacillus subtilis* or *Trichoderma harzianum* (2.5 g/L).
  - Copper octanoate soap or potassium bicarbonate sprays.
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Preventive**: Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L) or Mancozeb 75% WP (2 g/L) every 7–10 days.
  - **Curative**: Difenoconazole 25% EC (0.5 ml/L), Azoxystrobin 23% SC (1 ml/L), or Tebuconazole 25.9% EC (1 ml/L) at lesion onset.

---

### 5.3 Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*)
- **Symptoms**: Large, irregular water-soaked greasy lesions turning dark brown to purplish-black; white fuzzy mold on leaf undersides during high humidity; rapid stem collapse and brown leathery fruit rot.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Immediately bag, uproot, and destroy severely infected plants to halt massive airborne spore dispersal.
  - Avoid late-afternoon overhead watering; maximize canopy spacing.
  - Plant late-blight resistant cultivars (e.g., Mountain Merit, Defiant PhR).
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Preventive**: Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L), Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L), or Copper Hydroxide (2.5 g/L).
  - **Curative/Systemic (Active Outbreak)**: Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (2.0 g/L), Dimethomorph 50% WP (1.0 g/L), Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb (2.5 g/L), or Mandipropamid 23.4% SC (0.8 ml/L).

---

### 5.4 Leaf Mold (*Passalora fulva* / *Cladosporium fulvum*)
- **Symptoms**: Pale green to bright yellow chlorotic patches on the upper leaf surface; corresponding olive-green to brownish velvety mold sporulation on the underside.
- **Cultural & Environmental Control (Frequent in Greenhouses/High Tunnels)**:
  - Improve airflow and ventilation using exhaust fans; keep relative humidity below 85%.
  - Maintain greenhouse nighttime temperatures above 18°C.
  - Water only at the base via drip lines; never wet foliage.
  - Prune lower canopy foliage to improve cross-ventilation.
- **Biological & Chemical Control**:
  - Potassium bicarbonate foliar spray (3–5 g/L) or *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens*.
  - Spray Chlorothalonil (2 g/L), Copper Hydroxide (2 g/L), Difenoconazole (0.5 ml/L), or Boscalid (0.5 g/L) at first symptom appearance.

---

### 5.5 Septoria Leaf Spot (*Septoria lycopersici*)
- **Symptoms**: Numerous small (1.5–3 mm) circular spots with dark brown margins and sunken grayish-white centers, peppered with tiny black fruiting specks (pycnidia); leaves yellow and wither.
- **Cultural & Preventive Control**:
  - Strip off all lower infected leaves as soon as first spots appear.
  - Stake and cage plants; mulch soil surface to eliminate rain splash from soil.
  - Do not cultivate or harvest while plants are wet with dew or rain.
  - Practice 3-year crop rotation; eradicate solanaceous nightshade weeds.
- **Chemical Control**:
  - **Preventive**: Chlorothalonil (2 g/L), Mancozeb (2 g/L), or Copper Oxychloride (2.5 g/L) applied every 7–10 days starting at transplanting.
  - **Systemic**: Azoxystrobin (1 ml/L) or Difenoconazole (0.5 ml/L).

---

### 5.6 Spider Mites (*Tetranychus urticae*)
- **Symptoms**: Fine pale yellow or white stippling on the upper leaf surface; foliage becomes bronzed, dry, and papery; fine silken webbing on leaf undersides and shoot tips.
- **Cultural & Physical Control**:
  - Spray undersides of leaves with a forceful stream of water to dislodge mites and destroy webbing.
  - Keep plants properly irrigated; dry, dusty conditions promote rapid mite outbreaks.
  - Remove heavily infested leaves, bag them, and destroy them immediately.
- **Biological Control**:
  - Release predatory mites (*Phytoseiulus persimilis* or *Neoseiulus californicus*).
  - Spray Neem oil (1–2% concentration) or insecticidal soaps (potassium salts of fatty acids), ensuring full coverage of leaf undersides.
- **Chemical Control (Miticides)**:
  - Spiromesifen 22.9% SC (1 ml/L), Abamectin 1.9% EC (0.5 ml/L), or Bifenazate 50% SC (0.5 ml/L).
  - **Avoid broad-spectrum synthetic pyrethroids** (e.g. cypermethrin) which kill beneficial predatory mites and cause explosive resurgence.

---

### 5.7 Target Spot (*Corynespora cassiicola*)
- **Symptoms**: Brown circular lesions that develop pinpoint concentric rings; leaves yellow and drop prematurely; sunken lesions with light brown centers on green and ripe fruit.
- **Cultural & Chemical Control**:
  - Avoid overhead irrigation and promote rapid canopy drying with wider row spacing (18–24 inches).
  - Prune lower leaves to reduce relative humidity near the soil.
  - Spray Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L) or Mancozeb (2 g/L) preventively.
  - Apply Azoxystrobin + Difenoconazole (1 ml/L), Fluxapyroxad + Pyraclostrobin, or Boscalid upon disease detection.

---

### 5.8 Tomato Mosaic Virus (ToMV)
- **Symptoms**: Light and dark green mosaic or mottling on leaves; leaf distortion, blistering, and "shoestring" or "fern-like" leaf malformations; plant stunting and internal browning of fruit.
- **Transmission**: Highly stable virus mechanically transmitted by hands, tools, clothing, grafting, and tobacco products.
- **Notice**: Viral infection; **no chemical cure** exists. Strict hygiene and sanitation are mandatory.
- **Sanitation & Hygiene Protocols**:
  - Wash hands thoroughly with soap and warm water or 20% non-fat milk solution before handling tomato plants.
  - Strictly prohibit the use of tobacco products near greenhouses and fields.
  - Disinfect pruning tools in 10–20% household bleach solution or trisodium phosphate (TSP) between plants.
  - Immediately rogue out and burn or deeply bury symptomatic plants.
- **Varietal Resistance**:
  - Plant certified ToMV-resistant hybrid seed varieties (marked with "ToMV" or "T").

---

### 5.9 Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)
- **Symptoms**: Upward curling and cupping of leaf margins; severe yellowing (chlorosis) along margins and interveinal areas; reduced leaf size; severe plant stunting and flower abortion (no fruit set).
- **Transmission**: Transmitted by the sweetpotato whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*).
- **Notice**: Viral infection; **no direct chemical cure**. Management centers on whitefly exclusion, vector suppression, and resistant hybrids.
- **Physical & Cultural Exclusion**:
  - Raise seedlings under 50-mesh insect-proof netting.
  - Deploy yellow sticky traps (15–20 traps/acre) to monitor and trap whiteflies.
  - Use reflective silver/aluminum plastic mulches in tomato beds to repel incoming whiteflies.
  - Immediately rogue out infected seedlings showing upward leaf cupping.
- **Vector Control (Whitefly)**:
  - Spray Neem oil (10,000 ppm at 2–3 ml/L) or insecticidal soap early.
  - Apply systemic insecticides at whitefly detection: Acetamiprid 20% SP (0.2 g/L), Dinotefuran 20% SG (0.3 g/L), Spiromesifen 22.9% SC (1 ml/L), or Flonicamid 50% WG (0.3 g/L).
- **Resistant Varieties**:
  - Plant TYLCV-resistant varieties bearing Ty-1, Ty-2, or Ty-3 resistance genes.

---

### 5.10 Healthy
- **Status**: Tomato foliage is deep green, vigorous, and free from blight lesions, mites, or viral curling.
- **Action**: No remedy required. Maintain optimal drip irrigation, staking, and routine pest scouting.
